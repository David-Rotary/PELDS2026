How discovery works

Start with curiosity, not a checklist

People tend to act on answers they find for themselves. Rather than starting with another plan, start with a small, real experience that makes you stop and ask, "Why is that happening?" Every pathway above includes experiments built this way.

"I hadn't thought about our club that way before."That's the moment to aim for. Once your club has its own question, District people, other clubs, RCLs and Rotary resources become much more useful.
From stranger to member

Climb the engagement ladder

Hardly anyone joins cold. People join because they already know a member, or have worked alongside the club. A handy rule of thumb: fit the ask to the rung. Strangers get invited to do one thing; participants get invited to do a little more.

Say it well

Build your one-minute invite

Most people like the idea of Rotary but couldn't tell you what your club does. Fill in the blanks below and watch your invitation take shape — then copy it, tweak it, and make it sound like you.

Your invitation

Swap the insider words

Words that feel normal inside the club can sound like a locked door from outside.

District → our region's group of clubs
The Foundation → the giving arm that funds our projects worldwide
Fellowship → the people, and the friendships
Classification → what field or job you work in
Service Above Self → helping before asking
"Would you like to join?" → "Want to come along on Saturday?"
Why people give their time

Match the role to the reason

Research on volunteering finds people give time for six different reasons, and they tend to stay when what they do feeds the reason they came. Tap one or more to see roles that might fit. You could try it on yourself, a new member, or someone you're thinking of inviting.

The first year

Belonging before obligation

A good share of the members clubs lose leave in their first two years, and they usually drift away quietly rather than walk out. Here's one way to pace a new member's first year. Tap each stage to see who might own it.

Thriving or drifting?

The signs usually show weeks before anyone resigns. One sign on its own doesn't mean much. A pattern does, especially when someone stops saying "we" and starts saying "you".

Talk it over

Draw a conversation card

Great for the start of a board or committee meeting. Draw a card, give it ten minutes, and see where it goes. There are no right answers, only honest ones.
