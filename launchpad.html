3 Your club's goals in Rotary Club Central

Rotary now uses three-year rolling goals. The club's leadership team sets goals for three years ahead and reviews them every year. So your club may already have goals for your year. Your job is to review and update them with your incoming Board, not start from scratch.

  • Sign in at my.rotary.org → Rotary Club Central → choose your Rotary year.
  • Look at your club's history and trends first, then set goals you believe in.
  • The Club Excellence Award has 26 goals. Achieve at least 13 and report them in Club Central by 30 June. Six are priority goals:
Service participation
Club membership
Strategic plan
Annual Fund giving
PolioPlus giving
Benefactors

Not sure what you can edit, or when? Ask your Assistant Governor or the District Secretary.

Someone thinks you'd be great at this

If you've been sent this page, someone in your Rotary club has noticed what you bring. Here's what leading in Rotary is really like, and how you'd be supported. No pressure: just have a look.

It's not a job. It's a leadership course, with friends.

People pay thousands for programs that teach these things. In Rotary you learn them for a cause, with a team, and with support.

Run a roomChair meetings, keep them on track and make decisions stick.
Lead volunteersInfluence people who don't have to do what you say.
Grow other peopleShare the load instead of doing it all yourself.

Three jobs, not thirty

Set directionAgree two or three priorities and keep everyone pointed at them.
Build the teamA Board with clear roles, where the work is shared.
Look after peopleMembers feel welcome, useful and noticed.

You wouldn't be doing it alone

  • A mentor: usually the person who sent you this, or a past President.
  • Training: District Pre-PELDS and PELDS sessions before your year starts, with others in the same boat.
  • A toolkit: step-by-step pages, a plan you can build at your own pace, and practice conversations with an AI coach.
  • People to call: your Assistant Governor, District hubs and Rotary specialists.

Try it first, no commitment

Sit inCome along to a Board meeting or two and see what's involved.
Have a goChair one club meeting, or lead a small project with help.
Have a coffeeAsk the person who sent you this anything at all. No question is silly.
