The honest picture

Guess: how much has Australian Rotary membership fallen over twenty years?

It's not just us

Formal volunteering in Australia fell from about 36% of adults in 2019 to roughly 27–28% by 2024. It's a whole-of-sector problem, not a Rotary one.

Why people here think of leaving

Among ANZP members who had considered leaving: personal circumstances (48%) and cost (48%), both above the global average.

Why people here stay

91% stay to serve their local community, well above the global 62%. That's our biggest asset.

Our story

This is Sam.

Early forties. Moved into one of the newer estates a few years ago. Kids in the local footy club. Cares about the creek at the end of the street.

Sam has never been to a Rotary meeting, and won't read a brochure. Sam is exactly the person your club needs, and Sam will never walk in on their own.

Sam is made up, but you know a Sam.

The One Ask loop

Five moments turn a stranger into a Rotarian. Every one of them has a tool, and an owner, in your club.

Paint the picture. Sam is generous, busy, local and unasked. The loop on the right comes straight from our One Ask one-pager: Notice, Invite, Welcome, Belong, and only then Ask. We'll walk Sam round it, and at each stop I'll show you the tool we've built for that moment and who in the club owns it.
Moment 1 of 5
Notice
Someone who'd genuinely love this.

Sam is already in your orbit. Do you know where?

Know your community

A Community Insight Report maps your area: who lives there, what's changing, who's already doing good work. Berwick's showed an older established north (median age 43) and a younger growing south (35). Two communities, two different invitations.

Know the ten

Schools, businesses and groups your members already have a link to. A partnership brings ten or twenty people at once, and the relationships formed there become personal invitations later.

Phone or chatName one local group or business your club doesn't have a relationship with yet, but should.
Tools for this moment · owner: the whole club, led by the Board
Sam's footy club, Sam's kids' school, the creek Landcare group: these are where Sam already is. "The Ten You Should Know" helps a club list the organisations members already have a personal link to. The Berwick Community Insight Report is our worked example of mapping a community; any club can do one. Ask for chat answers and paste a few.

Invite
One thing. No pitch.

Don't explain Rotary. Invite Sam to one real thing.

Tools for this moment · owner: every member

Not ready to join? Perfect.

Sam goes on the Friends of the Club list: no dues, no meetings, just an open invitation to the next thing. Most people aren't ready the first time. Without this list, you lose almost all of them.

Tools for this moment · owner: the inviting member + Membership Chair
"What to Expect" is the only guide written for the prospect, not for us. It answers the questions people are too polite to ask: do I have to join? what does it cost? how much time? Clubs fill in their own answers. Friends of the Club takes one afternoon to set up and needs one named owner, not "the committee".
Moment 4 of 5
Belong
Check in, don't wait.

A third or more of the members clubs lose leave in their first two years.

They rarely storm out. They drift, because they never felt they belonged before the dues and rosters arrived. Belonging before obligation.

And the warmest list you're not using

Former members, Rotaractors, Interactors and youth program alumni already know what we do. They just haven't been asked, or the path wasn't obvious. That's The Second Yes.

Phone or chatWho's one person who left your club, or aged out of Rotaract, you could ring this month?
Fit the ask to the rung. Then the loop begins again: Sam becomes the next person who notices someone. Briefly mention The Second Yes: lapsed members usually left over a mismatch (time, cost, role), so the win-back offer has to be something different, not "we miss you, come back."
