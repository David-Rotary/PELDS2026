Running a club night: Grow your club, grow your people

A 90-minute conversation, not a talk. The club votes, talks at tables, and leaves with its own one-page plan. You don't need to be an expert, just willing to ask questions and keep time.

Rather not run it yourself? Ask for a facilitator from another club. A fresh face often gets more honest answers.

Two weeks before

  • Book a regular meeting night and call it a club conversation, not a membership talk
  • Ask the President or Secretary for three numbers: members today, how many joined and how many left in the last 12 months. The club snapshot works them out
  • Choose your scribe, and agree who'll own the club's “one change” afterwards
  • Tell members it's interactive and every voice counts. Invite your newest members especially

On the night: setting up

The room

  • Tables of four to six, not rows
  • A screen or TV, and a laptop with internet
  • A clicker if you have one

The laptop

  • Open the session, type the club's name and your name into the first slide (it remembers them)
  • Press F for full screen, N for notes, → to move on
  • The bottom bar has timers for table talk

Running order

Times add up to about 90 minutes. Parts marked optional can go if you're short of time.

Tips from the notes

Count what the room says, not the “right” answer. The point is an honest picture, not a perfect score.
The best idea in the room probably isn't on the slides. Leave space for it, and type it in.
Don't chase precision. On the health check, eyeball the average. The conversation matters more than the number.
Don't leave without a name and a date. The “one change” needs an owner and a “when”, or it won't happen.

Afterwards

  1. Download or print the summary from the last slide, and send it to the President and Secretary within a week
  2. Put the one change on the next Board agenda, with its owner. The Board pack has a ten-minute agenda item
  3. Whoever owns it: make it a commitment with a reminder on the drivers' kit page
  4. In 90 days, share what happened, so other clubs can borrow it
